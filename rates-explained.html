Understanding official, reference and parallel-market rates

Search for the “USD to NGN exchange rate” and you may see several different numbers. They are not necessarily wrong. They describe different things.

Official or reference rate

This is a rate published by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN). It works as a benchmark that others can compare against. NairaWise shows a CBN-sourced series relayed by the Frankfurter service, together with the rate date.

The CBN publishes more than one kind of exchange-rate information. NairaWise has not established that the series it shows is identical to the NFEM rate, so it does not use that label.

Parallel-market (street) rate

The parallel market is where people and bureaux de change trade foreign currency outside official channels. Its prices depend on the dealer, the city, the amount and the time of day. A single “parallel rate” on a website is therefore a snapshot from one source, not an official figure.

Your dealer’s quote

The rate you are quoted by a bank, bureau de change or online platform is the one that matters for your transaction. It may include a margin or fees. Compare more than one quote where you can.

Why the numbers differ

  • Different providers measure different things (central bank reference, bank offer, street price).
  • Rates change over time and some sources update only once per business day.
  • Buy and sell prices are different, and fees are extra.
  • Local supply and demand for dollars varies by place and moment.

Which should I use?

  • Budgeting or a rough idea: the reference rate is a sensible starting point.
  • Paying or receiving a real transaction: use the dealer’s written or spoken quote through “My own rate”.
